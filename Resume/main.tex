\section{Conferences and Publications}

\textbf{Agrawal, N.} Crisis, Compassion, and Capital: The Economics of Global Charitable Giving. \textit{To be presented at the Singapore Economics Review Conference (SERC) 2026}, Singapore.\\
{\small Do major crises create new charitable capital, or do they primarily reallocate existing donor attention? Using nearly 50,000 projects across 201 countries on GlobalGiving (2003–2025, totaling over \$575 million), we exploit the Russian invasion of Ukraine as a natural experiment. Difference-in-differences estimates reveal that Ukraine-related projects surged dramatically; approximately 60–70\% reflects genuine additionality (new donors entering the market) while 30–40\% reflects substitution from other causes. Within the disaster-response theme, non-Ukraine projects experienced a 40\% funding decline. Projects lacking emotionally salient or urgency-driven narratives are disproportionately crowded out as the attention environment shifts toward the dominant crisis narrative. Our findings establish that charitable markets are attention-constrained systems where crisis salience reshapes both the level and distribution of philanthropic capital.}

\vspace{5pt}

\textbf{Agrawal, N.} (2026). Phase-Aware Deep Learning with Complex-Valued CNNs for Audio Signal Applications. \textit{To be presented at the IEEE Conference on Artificial Intelligence (CAI) 2026}, Granada, Spain.\\
{\small Proposed complex-valued CNN architectures and training adaptations (complex batch-norm, initialization, and various complex activations) to exploit phase information often ignored by real-valued nets; evaluated in three stages: image baselines, MFCC audio classification, and phase-aware GNNs. Results indicated complex architectures can match real CNN baselines while offering improved handling of phase in audio pipelines, with analysis of trade-offs in stability versus expressivity across different complex activation functions.}

\vspace{5pt}

\textbf{Agrawal, N.}, Shariffdeen, R., Wang, G., Rasnayaka, S., \& Iyer, G. N. (2025). Analysis of Student-LLM Interaction in a Software Engineering Project. \textit{IEEE/ACM International Workshop on Large Language Models for Code (LLM4Code), ICSE 2025}, Ottawa, Canada.\\
{\small Tracked 126 undergraduate students over a 13-week semester and analyzed conversational logs, 730 LLM-generated code snippets, and code complexity metrics including lines of code, cyclomatic complexity, control flow graph depth, and Halstead effort. Demonstrated that conversational ChatGPT interactions produce shorter, lower-complexity code compared to Copilot, highlighting how dialogue-based prompting reduces code structural complexity while still meeting project requirements.}

\section{Working Papers}

\textbf{Agrawal, N.} Equilibrium Analysis for Strategic Information Revelation in Data Markets.\\
{\small Developed a tractable signalling model where data sellers choose how much of a dataset to disclose and buyers update beliefs accordingly. Derived conditions for pooling versus separating equilibria and used simulations to demonstrate how disclosure costs and prior beliefs shape market vulnerability to adverse selection. Highlighted policy implications, including standardized disclosure requirements, certification mechanisms, and third-party audits to improve allocation efficiency.}

\vspace{5pt}

\textbf{Agrawal, N.} Rigidity as a Signal: A Game-Theoretic Explanation for Persistent Vacancies in Urban Real Estate Markets.\\
{\small Modeled vacancy as a deliberate, costly signal in a two-period game with heterogeneous sellers, deriving multiple equilibrium types including pooling-holdout, pooling-discount, and both separating forms. Analyzed comparative statics and demonstrated how targeted policy levers such as vacancy taxes and price-ratio mandates can reshape equilibria toward more informative outcomes and improve allocative efficiency in urban housing markets.}
